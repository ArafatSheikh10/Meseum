The Museum of the Bible is one of the most innovative and interactive museums in the United States. Located near the U.S. Capitol in Washington, D.C., the museum is dedicated to exploring the history, narrative, and cultural impact of the Bible across the world. Opened in 2017, the museum spans six floors and features ancient artifacts, immersive theaters, digital experiences, rare manuscripts, educational exhibits, and family-friendly attractions. The museum welcomes visitors from all backgrounds and beliefs.

About Museum

Museum History

The Museum of the Bible was established by the Green family and officially opened on November 17, 2017. The museum was created to preserve and showcase the Bible’s historical significance and its influence on civilization, literature, art, and culture. The institution houses thousands of biblical artifacts and manuscripts dating back over 4,000 years.

Museum Tours

The museum offers several guided and self-guided tours for visitors:

Christian Faith Tour

A faith-centered tour explaining the formation and influence of the Bible throughout history.

World of Jesus of Nazareth Tour

An immersive experience that recreates a first-century Galilean village where visitors can explore the culture and daily life during the time of Jesus.

Exhibits & Attractions

The Impact of the Bible

On Floor 2, explore Bible in the World, where guests can explore the Bible’s impact on fashion, literature, music, and more, and Bible in America, with artifacts tracing the Bible’s profound influence on American culture.

The History of the Bible

On Floor 4, encounter hundreds of rare and beautiful artifacts as you discover the Bible’s history, from handwritten scrolls to mobile devices, as it was embraced by many communities with different traditions! 

Dead Sea Scrolls Exhibition

Displays ancient biblical manuscripts and historical texts.

The People of the Land: History and Archaeology of Ancient Israel

On Floor 5, see fascinating archaeological discoveries from the Israel Antiquities Authority and learn more about life in ancient Israel in this long-term exhibit.
